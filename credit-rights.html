Credit reports: accuracy, disputes, and red flags.

Consumers have important rights—but no legitimate company can lawfully erase accurate negative information simply because it is unfavorable.

7 minute readSources: FTC & CFPBReviewed August 2026
Non-credit-repair disclaimer: Valverde Group Inc. is not a credit repair organization. It does not dispute consumer-report items for clients, sell score improvement, promise deletion, or accept payment for credit repair services.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains that consumers have the right to dispute information they believe is inaccurate or incomplete with both the consumer reporting company and the company that supplied the information. The investigation is generally conducted without charge.

Accuracy—not a manufactured score

A dispute process is designed to address information that is inaccurate or incomplete. It is not a lawful method for removing accurate obligations. Avoid anyone advising you to make false identity-theft reports, misrepresent facts, create a new identity, or dispute every item regardless of accuracy.

Recognize credit-repair warning signs

The Federal Trade Commission states that the Credit Repair Organizations Act prohibits deceptive representations, restricts advance payment, requires written contracts, and provides cancellation rights for covered services.

  • Guaranteed score increases or approvals
  • Promises to remove accurate information
  • Pressure to pay before promised work is performed
  • Instructions to misstate identity or file false reports
  • Advice to avoid official communications or create a new credit identity

Use authoritative channels

Request reports through authorized sources, preserve copies and supporting records, communicate accurately, and follow the current instructions of the relevant consumer reporting company and information furnisher. If an issue remains unresolved, the CFPB provides a complaint process.
