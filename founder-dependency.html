Field Note 001

The founder-dependency test.

Five questions that reveal whether the organization can operate without constant founder intervention.

5 minute readOperational resilienceAugust 2026

Founder involvement is not automatically a weakness. Early companies often benefit from speed, judgment, and close customer relationships. Dependency becomes a risk when important work cannot move, information cannot be found, or commitments cannot be honored without one person.

1. Can the team explain the current priorities?

If every decision requires an interpretation from the founder, strategy has not been translated into operating choices.

2. Can someone find the essential records?

Contracts, account access, obligations, customer histories, vendor relationships, and operating instructions should not exist only in one inbox or memory.

3. Can routine decisions move within defined limits?

Document decision rights, approval thresholds, escalation conditions, and who acts when the usual decision-maker is unavailable.

4. Can customer delivery continue?

Identify where the founder personally holds expertise, relationships, or quality standards that have never been transferred into the organization.

5. Can the business review its own performance?

A resilient company can see commitments, cash position, delivery status, exceptions, and key results without assembling a new report from scratch.

The goal is not to remove the founder. It is to make the founder’s judgment teachable, transferable, and focused where it creates the most value.

Start reducing the risk

Choose one recurring founder intervention each week. Define the outcome, capture the decision logic, assign an owner, establish limits, and review the result. Small transfers of clarity compound into institutional capability.

Quick score

Count how many routine questions only one person can answer. Track the number monthly.
