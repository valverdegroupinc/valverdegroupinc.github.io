Field Note 003

Where AI actually belongs.

Use artificial intelligence where it expands human capacity without hiding responsibility, weakening privacy, or turning uncertain output into an unquestioned decision.

6 minute readAI & OperationsAugust 2026

The strongest AI use cases usually begin with a specific work problem, not a desire to “add AI.” Define the delay, cost, inconsistency, or capacity constraint first.

Good starting zones

  • Drafting and transforming low-risk internal content
  • Summarizing material a qualified person will verify
  • Classifying routine requests for human review
  • Retrieving approved organizational knowledge
  • Preparing structured data from non-sensitive documents
  • Generating alternatives that support—not replace—judgment

Higher-risk zones

Employment, credit, legal, tax, healthcare, safety, regulated communications, sensitive personal data, binding commitments, and financial decisions require stronger governance and appropriate professional review. Applicable obligations vary by use, industry, and location.

Ask seven questions

  1. What exact decision or task is being supported?
  2. Who remains accountable for the result?
  3. What data enters the system, and are we authorized to use it?
  4. How will output be verified?
  5. What happens when the system is wrong?
  6. Can the process be explained and audited?
  7. Does the measurable benefit justify the risk and maintenance?
The right AI system should make responsibility clearer—not provide somewhere for responsibility to disappear.

Adopt with evidence

Begin with a controlled pilot using appropriate data. Establish a baseline, monitor errors and exceptions, document the human review point, and decide in advance what would cause the pilot to stop.

AI belongs where it improves a well-understood system. It cannot compensate for missing ownership, poor records, or an undefined standard of quality.

First pilot

Choose a reversible internal task with low sensitivity and a qualified reviewer.
